What is exact here

Exact: every pixel is a light ray traced through Schwarzschild spacetime, so the shadow, the bent disk, the Einstein ring and the colour shifts follow general relativity for a black hole that does not spin. Your fall is a true geodesic on your own clock, computed in coordinates that stay regular at the horizon.

Simplified: the disk is a thin sheet of gas on circular orbits ending at 3 rs, brightness is compressed to fit a screen, the stars are generated and not real ones, and the picture stops just above the centre.

Realism switches: the gas inside the inner edge is given a fixed temperature and thins as it falls, turbulence is shaped to look like the real thing and is not simulated, and the lens effects imitate a camera, not the physics. Light-travel delay and limb darkening follow from the paths of the light.

Camera shake: its strength follows the ram pressure of the disk's gas on the ship and the tidal load on its hull, both worked out from your path. Three figures are assumed: a ship 20 m long, a hull that flexes in 0.2 s, and a disk a twentieth as thick as it is wide. Its timing runs on your clock, not the ship's.

Distances are in rs, the horizon radius. Space, R, F and the arrow keys work too.
